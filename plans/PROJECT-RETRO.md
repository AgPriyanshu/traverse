# Traverse — Project Retrospective (Sprints 1–9)

**Dates:** 2026-09-17 → 2026-09-29 (13 days, 9 sprints, 4 parallel agents per sprint)
**Goal:** upload a novel PDF, get a character knowledge graph with page-exact evidence on every relationship — extending to series, human review, evals, spoiler safety, and production ops.
**Outcome:** The core product works and is real, not demoed-around: page-exact citations, a temporally-correct evidenced graph, a human review loop that measurably prevents silent data corruption, zero measured spoiler leakage, and a working (if not yet publicly deployed) production posture. Several PRD-stated quality bars remain honestly unmeasured — not because they failed, but because two structural gaps (no frontier judge key provisioned, no multi-book gold-labelled series project to reconcile against) block the harnesses that would produce the numbers. This file says exactly which is which; `traverse-prd.md` §1.3 does too, cell by cell.

This is not a ninth retro — each sprint has its own, and this file does not repeat their detail. It answers the questions a single sprint retro can't: what shipped end to end, what pattern repeated across all nine sprints rather than one, and what a next owner needs to know that isn't findable by reading any single sprint's file.

## 2. The measured results that actually matter (real infra, real corpus, cited to their sprint)

- **The headline result — two Catherines kept apart** (`plans/sprint-3/RETRO.md` §2d, live re-ingestion, 2026-09-26): Catherine Earnshaw (major, 63 mentions) and Catherine Linton (protagonist, 167 mentions) are two correct nodes, split on the birth-date cue in the prose. Roster recall 1.000 on both novels; precision 0.900 (P&P) / 0.789 (Wuthering Heights) against a 0.90 target — the WH shortfall is a named, understood category (married/maiden-name identity links needing information the surface text doesn't state), not a new bug, and a real textual counter-example already ruled out guessing at that class of link.
- **Relation extraction hits a real recall ceiling, reported not hidden** (`plans/sprint-4/RETRO.md`): precision 0.71–1.0, recall 0.24–0.33 across P&P, best F1 0.426. Root cause: median chunk length is 60 characters, and several gold predicates were never proposed by the extractor in any run — this is the single most important open technical debt in the project, and no later sprint revisited it.
- **Router accuracy 86.9%** (`plans/sprint-6/HANDOFF.md`, 61 hand-labelled questions, live vLLM): `aggregation` recall 100% (the failure mode that would hallucinate a list, avoided outright); `path` recall 66.7% is the one real weak spot, with a documented consequence (under-serves rather than hallucinates).
- **Spoiler leakage: 0/22 checks** (`plans/sprint-8/RETRO.md` §2, re-verified live during this project retrospective's own merge-train smoke test): a project's graph genuinely shrinks (45→25 nodes, 24→3 edges in the live smoke test) when scoped to an earlier reading position. The most cleanly, repeatedly proven number in the whole project.
- **Gold question set: 63 questions, all six router classes, two novels** (`plans/sprint-8/RETRO.md`), exceeding the ≥60 portfolio target.
- **Worker-kill restart safety: proven with real container kills**, not simulation (`plans/sprint-7/RETRO.md` §2, `plans/sprint-9/RETRO.md` §1 — do1's chaos-test suite killed `celery-worker`, `db`, and `api` on the live shared stack and confirmed zero state loss on all three).
- **Routing policy live-switch meets its literal PRD acceptance criterion**: a `PUT /ops/routing-policy` changes the very next call in the same process, no restart — verified live during this retrospective (`version: 0 → 1`, immediately reflected on the next `GET`).

## 3. What's real but not yet measured — and exactly why

`traverse-prd.md` §1.3 now carries this cell by cell; this section is the short version. Two structural gaps account for nearly everything still open:

1. **No frontier judge key is configured anywhere in this environment.** `FRONTIER_MODEL`/`FRONTIER_API_KEY` are blank in `.env` by design (no egress path exists without a human supplying a real key). This blocks: answer accuracy, citation precision, abstention rate against the 63-question gold set (Sprint 6/8), real ECE for Sprint 8's calibration mechanism (verified on synthetic data only — `ece_before=0.200`, `ece_after=0.000`/`0.083`, but zero real `CorrectionFeedback` rows exist because Sprint 7's review queue has never been exercised by an actual reviewer in this environment), and most of the ablation table's accuracy column. **This is the single highest-leverage thing a human can do to unlock real numbers across three sprints' worth of otherwise-honest gaps.**
2. **No multi-book, gold-labelled series project exists to measure cross-book reconciliation against.** `Wuthering Heights` and `Pride and Prejudice` are both gold-labelled as standalone novels; the reconciliation-quality endpoint (`/ops/reconciliation-quality`) is live and real but returns `gold_available: false` for every project checked, because no project combines two books from the same gold-labelled series. Building one (or reusing the two novels as if they were volumes of one series, if that's defensible) would unlock the three cross-book reconciliation metrics in §1.3 that have been open since Sprint 5.

Everything else carried forward in `traverse-prd.md` §1.3 traces back to one of these two gaps, or to a metric (B³ F1 specifically, full-novel wall-clock at exactly 350pp, p95/TTFT against the literal PRD bar) that was simply never re-measured after the harness that would produce it moved on to the next sprint's work — not fabricated, just not revisited.

## 4. Patterns that repeated across most or all of nine sprints

These are the things worth a future team (or a future version of this same process) knowing, because they are not sprint-specific bugs — they are properties of *this way of building software* (four parallel agents, a human orchestrator, git worktrees, a merge train).

1. **The merge-train gate catches bugs no per-branch test suite can, every single sprint.** Sprint 1 found 4 integration-only bugs this way; Sprint 2 found 4 more; Sprint 3's real-book run found things no fixture novel could. This is not a fluke — every sprint's own retro independently rediscovers the same lesson (Sprint 1's retro even predicts it: "this is why Day 5's demo step exists as a mandatory gate rather than 'tests passed, ship it'"). **The mechanism, not the specific bugs, is the reusable finding**: run the real, cold, merged stack before calling a sprint done, every time, without exception.
2. **Shared append-only docs (`HANDOFF.md`, `SCR.md`, `STANDUP.md`) produce an add/add conflict every sprint, and SCR numbering collides independently across agents every sprint too**, because branches diverge from a common ancestor before any agent sees another's filings. Sprint 1 named this; Sprint 2 confirmed a proposed fix (seeding the files as empty tracked files at the freeze) wouldn't even have prevented the *numbering* collision specifically; by Sprint 7–9 this had become a five-minute, unremarkable merge-train step (concatenate, renumber, fix cross-references) rather than a surprise. **Never fixed at the root. Worth fixing for a Sprint 10, if there is one** — a numbering scheme keyed to agent+sprint (e.g. `be1-1`, `do1-3`) rather than a shared incrementing counter would remove the collision class entirely.
3. **Cross-story dependencies on the same underlying table/mechanism, built by two agents who can't see each other's unfinished code, happen almost every sprint and are resolved the same way each time: a same-day fast-follow after the main merge train, not a blocked sprint close.** Sprint 8: do1's ablation runner needed be2's config-switch, which landed after do1's branch was cut — closed with a fast-follow the same day. Sprint 9: be1's session-isolation work and do1's upload-guard work both independently built session-creation/quota/sweep logic against the same `UploadSession` table — closed with a same-day fast-follow reconciling them. **This pattern works and should keep being used** — but it argues for tighter story-level interface contracts at the Day-1 freeze specifically when two agents will plausibly touch the same table, not just the same migration, which the freeze process doesn't currently check for.
4. **An agent's own "all clean" build/test report is necessary but not sufficient evidence.** Sprint 9 caught this directly: fe1 reported `pnpm tsc --noEmit` clean, and it wasn't — two real compile errors (`export type { X }` re-exporting without locally binding the name; an ambient `declare module` interface-merge conflict with a third-party package) were only caught because the orchestrator independently rebuilt and reran the test image rather than trusting the report. **Kept practice, worth keeping**: an independent merge-train verification pass per branch, even under explicit pressure to move fast.
5. **Shared-host resource contention (concurrent agents' `docker compose` activity recreating shared `db`/`rabbitmq` containers mid-test-run) produced transient test failures in nearly every sprint from Sprint 3 onward**, and the correct response — reproduce in isolation, confirm clean, don't chase it as a code regression — was applied consistently rather than papered over by retrying blindly. The one exception is worth naming: a genuinely recurring flake (`web/tests/review-speed.test.tsx`, timing out at ~5.0–5.1s against a 5000ms Vitest default under load, three separate times across Sprints 7–9) was *not* a pure environment flake — it was a real, fixable test-timeout-too-tight bug that happened to look identical to the environment noise every other time, and was only fixed for real during this project retrospective's complete-testing pass, not any single sprint's own verification. **Worth the discipline of asking "does this fail the same way every time, or just sometimes" before writing off a repeat flake** — this one repeated because it was structurally borderline, not because the host was noisy that particular day.
6. **The rate-limit interruption is the expected shape of a multi-agent sprint, not an anomaly**, confirmed as early as Sprint 1's retro and recurring through every sprint after. The fix that held — commit as soon as a story is real and tested, not when the whole sprint's stories are done — was explicitly instructed to every agent starting Sprint 7 onward and worked: no sprint after that lost a completed story to an interruption, only in-flight work that was recoverable by resuming from the worktree's actual `git status` rather than restarting from scratch.
7. **Real bugs were found by real infrastructure almost every sprint, not by code review.** A partial list, spanning the whole project: a ~1-in-8 Docling cold-start page-drop (Sprint 1); a stale-event-loop bug hitting both the LLM semaphore (Sprint 4) and the Neo4j driver (Sprint 5) independently, from the same Celery-per-task-is-its-own-event-loop root cause; Qwen3-8B's reasoning mode silently multiplying cost 60x before being found and disabled (Sprint 3); a `greenlet_spawn` production bug in the query-persistence path, flagged in Sprint 8 and fixed for real in Sprint 9; a real cross-project content-hash pooling bug in the upload-reingest fallback (Sprint 9). **None of these were caught by a unit test written in advance** — every one was found by running the real system against real or adversarial conditions (a real book, a real container kill, a real concurrent upload). This is the single strongest argument for the project's own standing rule to prefer real infrastructure over mocks in tests, and it held up under scrutiny across nine sprints, not just as a stated principle.

## 5. What's carried forward for a human, specifically

Not agent-shaped work — decisions or actions that need a person, not a story:

1. **Provision a real `FRONTIER_MODEL`/`FRONTIER_API_KEY`.** This alone unlocks: real answer accuracy and citation precision against the 63-question gold set, real ECE for Sprint 8's calibration mechanism (once Sprint 7's review queue is also exercised by a real reviewer — see next item), most of the ablation table's accuracy column, and the frontier axis of Sprint 9's routing-policy demo.
2. **Have a real reviewer exercise Sprint 7's review queue against real pending tasks** (three real `merge_characters` tasks are open right now, confirmed live during this retrospective's smoke test) — this is what gives Sprint 8's calibration mechanism something real to fit on, closing a gap open since Sprint 8.
3. **Decide on public deployment** (Sprint 9's S9.4): domain, cloud host, DNS, TLS cert, and accepting the real cost/security exposure of a public URL. Tooling is built and locally verified (`docker-compose.prod.yml`, `docker/nginx/edge.conf`, `scripts/verify_prod_deploy.py`); exact go-live steps are in `plans/sprint-9/HANDOFF.md`.
4. **The 90-second recording** (Sprint 9's S9.14) — needs a live, screen-recorded walkthrough of the demo script; not producible from inside an agent session.
5. **The engineering writeup** (Sprint 9's S9.15, "Why two-pass extraction, with numbers") — orchestrator-owned, not yet drafted as of this file; a natural next artifact once the numbers in §2/§3 above are final.
6. **Decide whether to build a real extraction-mode switch and a second gold-labelled series project**, or accept the ablation table and cross-book reconciliation metrics as permanently out of scope for this portfolio piece — both are real, bounded, buildable gaps, not open-ended ones, but neither is free.
7. **Write the missing Sprint 5 and Sprint 6 retros**, or explicitly decide they're not worth backfilling now that the project is otherwise closed out — named as a gap in this file rather than silently left unaddressed.

## 6. One paragraph, if someone reads nothing else

Traverse's core promise — page-exact, evidenced, temporally-correct character relationships, including telling two same-named characters apart across a whole novel — is real and independently re-verified on a cold, fully-merged run, not just claimed. Its weakest measured number (relation extraction recall, capped around 0.3) is honestly reported with a correct architectural diagnosis rather than hidden or tuned around. Its newest safety property (spoiler leakage) is its most cleanly proven, at zero across 22 real checks. What's missing is not broken engineering — it is two structural gaps (a frontier judge key, a second gold-labelled series project) that no agent can close alone, and three pieces of work (public deployment, a recorded demo, the writeup) that were deliberately left for a human because they are that kind of decision, not that kind of task.
